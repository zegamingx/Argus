# Roadmap and Technology Research Register

**Review date:** 2026-11-05 · **Status:** Planning baseline · **Branch:** main

## 1. Accepted product direction

Argus follows **Local Knowledge Genesis** and **One Origin, Independent Evolution**:

- Each installation creates its own model parameters locally.
- First launch performs model construction and initialization, then opens the application.
- General-corpus learning is a resumable background process, not a first-launch blocking task.
- User-selected local dataset import is the primary general-corpus intake; bundled Small C4 and autonomous web acquisition are not prerequisites.
- Personal learning adapts to the user only through explicitly authorized data.
- Model configuration follows device capability; training workload follows live device conditions.
- The initial neural architecture remains subject to research.
- No central training server, federated training, cross-user model sharing or automatic model synchronization is planned.
- Each instance may later acquire permitted public knowledge for its own local use; this is an optional network capability. User-imported local datasets are the primary initial general-corpus path.

## 2. Project delivery phases

The Phase 0–6 labels below describe the **product delivery and implementation sequence**. They are distinct from the numbered research stages used in documents 33–35 and in research-register statuses such as R-010, R-015 and R-026.

### Phase 0 — Documentation and research (current)
G0's research scope and evidence controls are closed under ADR-024, and G2's four-family architecture comparison shortlist is accepted under ADR-025 in [01 — System Requirements and Decisions](01_SYSTEM_REQUIREMENTS_AND_DECISIONS.md) and [35 — Android Architecture Evidence and Component Comparison Matrix](35_ANDROID_ARCHITECTURE_EVIDENCE_AND_COMPONENT_MATRIX.md). Maintain the source/build baseline and P0 audit. The Stage 3 feasibility synthesis and analytical resource envelope under [33 — From-Scratch Training Feasibility](33_FROM_SCRATCH_TRAINING_FEASIBILITY.md) are complete; E1–E7 remain future implementation/device verification. G2's four-family shortlist is accepted under ADR-025, and the G3 one-primary-disposition baseline is accepted under ADR-026; deferred architecture-specific values remain open. Continue G1-D tokenizer closure under [34 — Tokenizer Evaluation and Decision Framework](34_TOKENIZER_EVALUATION_AND_DECISION.md) and prepare the G4 comparative design synthesis under [35 — Android Architecture Evidence and Component Comparison Matrix](35_ANDROID_ARCHITECTURE_EVIDENCE_AND_COMPONENT_MATRIX.md). G1-D blocks token-sensitive choices until the device-conditioned tokenizer profile portfolio and its selection contract are ready; the target is not one universal vocabulary for every phone. Keep the user-import workflow, Genesis lifecycle, device-adaptive profiles and tokenizer/model contracts as documented implementation dependencies. Argus does not perform source-by-source online rights research for user-selected imports; the user makes operation-specific authority declarations. Public-source candidate lists are optional future acquisition research, not initial import gates. No source implementation, build, device experiment or workflow execution belongs to this phase unless separately authorized. External papers and benchmarks are evidence for existing product research, not permission to expand scope or reverse accepted decisions.

### Phase 1 — Architecture design decision review (not started)
Select a **pre-implementation architecture design** after the Stage 3 published-evidence/analytical baseline in document 33, documentary tokenizer G1-D in document 34, the reviewed component-level evidence in document 35, and the cross-family synthesis in document 38. The output must define a bounded portfolio of device-conditioned model/tokenizer configurations, including the deterministic profile-selection policy and per-generation tokenizer identity, rather than one universal parameter/vocabulary configuration. E1–E7 physical measurements remain later verification of implementation support, not a prerequisite for design selection. The output is a versioned design contract and ADR that explicitly lists unverified implementation assumptions; it is not a claim of measured Android support or proven general-purpose from-scratch feasibility. Resolve architecture family/modules, initialization contracts, tokenizer-dependent interfaces, training objective/optimizer semantics, model identity, device-profile assumptions, migration boundaries and open risks. The tokenizer fixture should be sourced from a user-imported local dataset with an explicit declaration for tokenizer fitting and/or aligned development. Public-source acquisition and catalog-level rights research are not prerequisites for tokenizer experiments. Record architecture decisions in ADRs without silently closing corpus, device or implementation verification gates.

### Phase 2 — Implementation planning (not started)
Create a dependency-ordered engineering plan with acceptance criteria, numerical tests, Android lifecycle tests, migration and rollback. Resolve how the current Kotlin prototype is isolated, replaced or retained for limited experiments. No broad implementation begins before P0 remediation is reviewed.

### Phase 3 — Genesis foundation implementation (not authorized)
Implement device profiling, the architecture/configuration portfolio, tokenizer profile registry and deterministic selection, bounded precomputed/local tokenizer preparation, module initialization policies, model identity, complete model-state persistence and first-launch readiness. Tokenizer preparation must finish or select an approved compatible fallback before vocabulary-dependent model tensors are allocated. No neural pretraining is required before application unlock.

### Phase 4 — General background learning (not authorized)
Implement tokenizer-bound ingestion of user-imported local corpora, tokenizer/runtime conformance, from-scratch neural training, scheduler, checkpoint/recovery and general evaluation. The tokenizer artifact is fixed by the Genesis generation contract; this phase does not mutate an active vocabulary. Public-source acquisition, local knowledge storage and retrieval remain a separate optional workstream. Background training begins only after initial Genesis commit and app readiness.

### Phase 5 — Personal learning (not authorized)
Implement inspectable memory, personal capture/experience intake under [48 — Personal Capture and Experience Intake Contract](48_PERSONAL_CAPTURE_AND_EXPERIENCE_INTAKE_CONTRACT.md), personal candidate consent/review, privacy controls, personal training and general-capability retention gates. Resolve the contract's PC-IMPL-01–PC-IMPL-07 bindings before enabling a capture surface or replacing/retiring the prototype AccessibilityService path.

### Phase 6 — Architecture evolution (future research)
Investigate parameter growth, module addition/replacement, architecture migration, validation and rollback. Structural evolution is not automatic and cannot publish an unvalidated candidate.

## 5. Technology radar

- **ADOPT:** selected for a defined scope with evidence.
- **TRIAL:** limited proof-of-concept planned or underway.
- **ASSESS:** investigate; no commitment.
- **HOLD:** blocked by maturity, access, licensing or resource limits.
- **RETIRE:** remove an existing path under a migration plan.

Tags apply to a specific version and platform, not globally to a vendor or architecture family.

## 6. Review cadence and exit conditions

Review SDKs before implementation decisions; hardware entries at least quarterly; Android API lifecycle at major platform releases; and corpus terms before every distribution revision. Record source URL, revision/publication date and last-checked date.

End the documentation phase only when the gate in [15 — Verification and Compatibility](15_VERIFICATION_COMPATIBILITY.md) passes. Completing a document or checking a roadmap item does not authorize coding, builds, CI workflows or artifact publication.
